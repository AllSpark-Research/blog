About AllSpark Research

Intelligence is field-tested, not defined.

AllSpark Research is Xiaohongshu's foundation model research team. We build foundation models, agentic systems, post-training methods, evaluation infrastructure, and production AI systems grounded in real-world community interactions.

This journal is our window to the research community. It is not a marketing channel and not a project summary. It is a record of what we observed, how we formalized it, what we tried, and what remains open.

We publish because hard problems in AI are better solved by a community that can see them clearly. The failures are as important as the results. The open problems are as important as the methods.

What We Believe

Three Core Commitments

01 —
Intelligence is field-tested.
Intelligence is not only the capability encoded in model parameters, and not the score on a leaderboard. Intelligence must be tested in real communities, real content, real tools, real feedback, and real constraints. A model that cannot operate in the wild has not yet demonstrated the intelligence we care about.
02 —
Evaluation is infrastructure.
Evaluation is not the acceptance step after training — it is the scientific instrument that defines what progress means. Benchmark, Rubric, Harness, LLM Judge, Human Calibration, and Online Feedback together constitute the measurement infrastructure that makes model evolution possible.
03 —
Agents evolve through interaction.
An agent is not a more complex workflow. It is a model operating in a closed loop: perceive, plan, act, observe, reflect, and update. Trajectories, failure cases, and real feedback are the fuel for the next generation of models. The loop is not a pipeline — it is a scientific posture.
Research Approach

How We Work

Our research cycle begins with field observation. We do not start from a theoretical gap in the literature — we start from a failure mode we encountered in production.

We then abstract the failure into a general research problem: something that could be formalized, evaluated, and trained on. We build the instrument before we build the method.

Finally, we close the loop: successful methods return to the field as updated model behavior, and the observation cycle begins again.

Editorial Standard

What We Publish and Why

Every article must pass a seven-dimension rubric before publication. The dimensions are: generality (does it ask a transferable research question?), field-groundedness (does it come from a real scenario?), formalization (is there a clear problem model?), evaluability (is there a measurement approach?), failure honesty (are failure modes named?), open problems (is there a clearly stated frontier?), and brand consistency.

What We Will Not Publish

Articles that only report improvements. Articles that use phrases like "significant upgrade," "comprehensive breakthrough," or "leading performance" as their primary claim. Articles that do not name a single failure mode or limitation. Articles that present business results without a general research question.

We do not claim our work is a general solution. We do not hide the cases where it fails. We believe that intellectual honesty is the only foundation on which long-term technical credibility can be built.
